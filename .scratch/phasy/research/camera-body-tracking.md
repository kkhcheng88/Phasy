# Can a phone-browser camera place a marker on a back within 2 cm?

## 1. Summary

Verdict: **uncertain** for pure markerless pose estimation to hit ~2 cm reliably on the back; **likely** if the setup adds a physical fiducial (sticker/ArUco/AprilTag) on the skin, or accepts a looser tolerance for pure landmark interpolation. No model tested here outputs a trunk/back landmark — all output only shoulder and hip points on the torso, so any back-of-torso marker (e.g. mid-upper-trapezius) is an *interpolated guess*, not a detected point. No study found measures back-view keypoint error in cm; nearest evidence is front/side-view keypoint error (pixels or PCK) and one AR-anatomy-adjacent smartphone study (needle guidance) reporting 5.1 mm on muscle under camera+surface tracking, not pose landmarks.

Recommended test setup: shirt off, Receiver seated or prone, Helper's phone at ~1–1.5 m, normal indoor room light, MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (or BlazePose via TF.js) running in-browser; compare interpolated shoulder-hip trapezius point against a ruler-measured true point on ≥15 people, ≥3 poses (seated, prone, slight turn) each. Run a second arm of the same test with a small printed sticker/ArUco marker at the target point as an accuracy ceiling reference.

## 3. Accuracy facts

- BlazePose paper reports PCK@0.2 (percentage of correct keypoints within 20% of torso size) of 84.1% (BlazePose Full, "AR" dataset) and 84.5% (yoga dataset), versus OpenPose's 87.8% / 83.4% on the same two datasets; human-annotator baseline PCK@0.2 was 97.2% — https://arxiv.org/html/2006.10204v1 . This is a percentage-of-keypoints-correct-within-threshold metric, not a direct pixel/cm error, and the threshold itself (20% of torso size) is large — e.g. for an ~40 cm visible torso that threshold is ~8 cm, far looser than our 2 cm target. No cm or pixel RMS error figure was found in the fetched excerpt (unverified beyond PCK).
- No source found reporting shoulder- or hip-specific keypoint error in pixels, %, or cm for BlazePose, MoveNet, or MediaPipe Pose Landmarker specifically. Marked unverified.
- No source found with dedicated back-view (person facing away from camera) keypoint accuracy numbers for any of the three models. Marked unverified — this is a direct gap against the ticket's question.
- Craniovertebral-angle-based posture apps (front/side view, not back view) report measurement SEM of 1.56–2.21° and MDC of 4.32–6.12° for craniovertebral angle, and acromion-alignment SEM 0.3–1.396 units — a systematic review of photogrammetry posture apps, most using manual/AI-assisted 2D landmarks, not back-view — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12827935/ . These are angle/relative-position errors, not translatable directly to a cm error on a back marker (unverified as a proxy).
- Smartphone AR body-surface-tracking study (needle guidance, not pose-landmark based — uses Vuforia Model Target deep-learning surface tracking, not BlazePose/MoveNet): phantom median accuracy 4.8 mm (IQR 3.3–7.7 mm), in vivo mean 8.9 mm (SD ±4.3); organ-specific in vivo accuracy best in muscle at 5.1 mm — https://www.jvir.org/article/S1051-0443(24)01126-6/fulltext ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42229872/ (abstract not readable directly via web fetch; figures taken from the JVIR conference-abstract source, cross-check recommended — unverified pending direct read of the PubMed record). This is body-surface tracking for a full 3D anatomical overlay registered to a prior CT scan, not live pose-landmark interpolation, so it establishes a *best-case ceiling* for camera-based skin-point accuracy, not a like-for-like figure.

## 5. AR anatomy studies

- Smartphone AR, CT-based body-surface registration (Vuforia Model Target, deep-learning surface tracking, not a general pose-estimation model) for percutaneous needle guidance: phantom median 4.8 mm (IQR 3.3–7.7 mm) accuracy, 2.2° median angular error; in vivo mean 8.9 mm (SD ±4.3) accuracy, 4.2° (SD ±2.2) angular error; best organ-specific in vivo accuracy was muscle at 5.1 mm, vs. kidney 8.7 mm and liver 13.0 mm — https://www.jvir.org/article/S1051-0443(24)01126-6/fulltext . This is the closest found study to "projecting an anatomical position onto a live camera image of a real person," but it targets deep internal structures via a pre-existing patient CT scan and dedicated surface-tracking (not BlazePose/MoveNet/MediaPipe pose landmarks), and the target population/setup (interventional radiology, controlled clinical environment) differs substantially from a phone-in-hand home self-care scenario. Direct transfer to "locate mid-trapezius from pose landmarks alone" is not established.
- No other peer-reviewed study was found (in this research pass) that projects a specific muscle or trigger-point location onto a live phone-camera image of an undraped back using markerless pose estimation, with a reported cm-level accuracy. This is marked as an open gap, not as "does not exist" — search was not exhaustive (see §7).

## 6. Estimate and reasoning (not cited fact — the team's own judgment)

Pure markerless (pose-landmark-interpolation only): **unlikely** to consistently hit ~2 cm on a trigger point like mid-upper-trapezius. Reasoning: (a) none of the three browser-capable models output any landmark on the back/trunk surface itself — the target point is always synthesized from shoulder/hip landmarks plus an assumed body-proportion ratio, and that ratio varies with individual build, posture (seated vs. prone), and camera angle; (b) the only accuracy figures available for these models (PCK@0.2) use an error tolerance class around 15–20% of torso size, i.e. several cm on a real back, well above the 2 cm bar, and no dedicated back-view number exists to confirm or refute whether rear-facing accuracy is better or worse than that baseline; (c) independent rear-camera pose literature flags rear views as a harder case due to self-occlusion, though that literature is about a different camera geometry (egocentric rear-facing cameras, not a phone photographing someone's back) so it only weakly supports pessimism here.

Markerless + calibration step (e.g., Helper briefly taps/confirms the point once, or app asks for a reference object of known size in frame): **uncertain, plausibly closer to reach**, because per-user calibration removes the body-proportion-ratio error, leaving mainly camera/landmark jitter — but no study measured this scenario, so this is speculation, not a citable fact.

Marker-assisted (a small sticker or printed ArUco/AprilTag placed at or near the true point, located once by a clinician/self-palpation, then tracked frame-to-frame): **likely** to reach 2 cm or better, because the problem changes from "infer anatomy" to "detect a known visual pattern," which fiducial-marker and AR-registration literature shows can reach mm-to-low-cm accuracy in the closest available analogue (body-surface AR registration study, §5). This shifts the hard part from computer vision to correct initial placement by a human, which is a workflow/UX problem, not a CV accuracy problem.

Best test setup to actually answer the question: normal home light (not clinical), phone at 1–1.5 m, Receiver's shirt off, seated and prone poses, ≥15 varied-body-type subjects, ground truth marked with a ruler/tape from a known bony landmark (e.g., C7 or acromion) that a clinician can locate reliably; run both the markerless-interpolation arm and a sticker/ArUco arm side by side so the team has both a floor (pure landmarks) and ceiling (marker-assisted) number before committing to a build approach.

## 7. Open questions

- No source found with a direct pixel/cm/percent keypoint error specifically for BlazePose, MoveNet, or MediaPipe Pose Landmarker on shoulders or hips (only the looser PCK@0.2 metric was found). Needs either a primary benchmark paper with per-keypoint error tables, or our own measurement.
- No source found evaluating any of these three models specifically on a person facing away from the camera (back view). The rear-camera pose paper found (arXiv 2503.11652) is about a different camera geometry (head-mounted rear-facing cameras for egocentric self-pose), so its relevance to "phone facing someone's back" is unverified.
- iOS Safari-specific browser-version support and WebXR support/consistency for MediaPipe Tasks for Web and TF.js were not independently confirmed from Google's own docs in this pass (only a third-party Medium post and general TF.js repo claims were found) — needs a direct compatibility test on an actual iPhone.
- No peer-reviewed physiotherapy/rehab study was found that reports cm-level accuracy for placing a trigger-point or muscle marker on a live back image using markerless pose estimation. Search in this pass was time-boxed and not exhaustive; a deeper literature search (Google Scholar, PubMed with rehab-specific terms) may surface something not found here.
- The BAR-M AprilTag physiotherapy accuracy figures (§4) were taken from a ResearchGate figure caption, not the full original paper; the underlying paper should be located and read directly to confirm.
