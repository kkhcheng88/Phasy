# Trigger Point & Treatment Sources — Research (2026-09-24)

## 1. Summary

Nine target books plus one addition were verified against publisher/ISBN pages; all editions below are confirmed, none unverified. Open-access evidence exists for trigger-point reliability, cupping safety, and one ISO device-safety standard (ISO 5227:2022); no dedicated WHO cupping guideline was found. Strong red-flag and contraindication sources exist (IFOMPT, JOSPT international consensus, NICE NG59, AHA, NHS/Wells). Copyright: facts/ideas/anatomical locations are not protectable (Feist v. Rural); only the book's own original drawings, exact wording, and original selection/arrangement are — so the app must redraw diagrams and rewrite text in its own words, not trace or copy.

**Buy these 3 first:**
1. **Travell, Simons & Donnelly's Myofascial Pain and Dysfunction** (4th ed., 2025) — the primary clinical source all lay books derive from; needed to verify our own trigger-point maps are accurate before we redraw them.
2. **The Trigger Point Therapy Workbook** (Davies & Davies, 3rd ed., 2013) — already written for laypeople doing self/partner treatment; closest existing analogue to our app's step 2–3 flow.
3. **Trail Guide to the Body** (Biel, 6th ed., 2019) — best resource for teaching a layperson to find a muscle by touch (our step 2 "narrow down by palpation"), which neither of the above teaches well.

## 3. Open-Access Sources

- **Trigger point palpation reliability (systematic review):** Lucas, Rich, Polus, "A Systematic, Critical Review of Manual Palpation for Identifying Myofascial Trigger Points" — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003999308001433 (PubMed abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18503816/). Found only 2 of 11 studies were high quality; reliability of trigger-point identification by touch is weak-to-moderate.
- **Inter-rater agreement meta-analysis:** Barbero et al., "Interrater Agreement of Manual Palpation for Identification of Myofascial Trigger Points: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis" — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28098584/. Pooled κ = 0.452 (95% CI 0.364–0.540); localized tenderness (κ=0.676) and pain recognition (κ=0.575, i.e. the person confirming "yes, that's my familiar pain") were the most reliable single criteria — this directly supports the app's design choice to rely on the person's own pain-recognition report rather than the palpator's judgment of a nodule/taut band (least reliable, 33–90% agreement).
- **Shoulder-muscle inter-rater study:** "Interrater reliability of palpation of myofascial trigger points in three shoulder muscles" — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19066669/
- **Trunk/lower-limb inter-examiner study:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10724067/
- **Cupping effectiveness/safety, recent systematic review + meta-analysis:** "Update evidence of effectiveness on pain relieving of cupping therapy" (2024/2025) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11955767/ (PMC, fully open access).
- **Cupping for chronic musculoskeletal pain, systematic review + meta-analysis:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12121573/ (PMC, open access) — reports low/very-low-quality evidence that adverse-event incidence is low but non-zero.
- **Cupping evidence-mapping study:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10640990/ (PMC, open access).
- **Cupping adverse events, Korea systematic review:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259167373 / abstract also at https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S187638201300084X — reported adverse events include hematoma, local pain, erythema, edema, ecchymosis, factitial panniculitis; rarer: syncope, infection, scarring.
- **ISO standard — device safety, verified on official ISO page:** **ISO 5227:2022, "Traditional Chinese medicine — Safety controls for cupping devices"** — https://www.iso.org/standard/81034.html. Applies to cups made of manufactured material (plastic, glass, rubber, ceramic, silicone). Developed under ISO/TC 249 (Traditional Chinese medicine), led by Saudi FDA.
- Related device standards also on ISO/TC 249 (glass and air-extraction cupping devices, not device-safety-general): ISO 22213:2020 (glass cupping device) — https://www.iso.org/standard/72884.html; ISO 19611:2017 (air extraction cupping device) — https://www.iso.org/standard/65463.html.
- **WHO cupping-specific guideline:** not found. WHO has acupuncture point-location standards used by some cupping protocols, but no dedicated WHO clinical guideline for cupping was located — **unverified / likely does not exist as a standalone document**; do not cite a WHO cupping standard in-app.

## 4. Red Flags and Contraindications

All items below are screening prompts for referring a user to a clinician, not diagnoses.

**Spinal / musculoskeletal red flags**
- International expert-consensus framework for serious spinal pathology (cauda equina syndrome, spinal fracture, infection, malignancy) — Finucane et al., "International Framework for Red Flags for Potential Serious Spinal Pathologies," JOSPT 2020 — https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2020.9971 (open PDF: https://www.jospt.org/doi/pdf/10.2519/jospt.2020.9971). Plain-word flags to include: sudden loss of bladder/bowel control or saddle-area numbness (possible cauda equina — emergency); unexplained weight loss with back pain; back pain with fever; back pain after significant trauma or in someone with known osteoporosis/long-term steroid use (possible fracture); night pain that doesn't ease lying down and gets steadily worse (possible malignancy/infection).
- IFOMPT framework for neck/vascular risk before manual neck treatment — "International Framework for Examination of the Cervical Region for Potential of Vascular Pathologies of the Neck," JOSPT 2022 — https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2022.11147 (official PDF: https://www.ifompt.org/site/ifompt/IFOMPT%20Cervical%20Framework%20final%20September%202020.pdf). Plain-word flags: sudden, severe "worst-ever" headache or neck pain; double vision, slurred speech, dizziness/fainting, or facial numbness/weakness alongside neck pain; neck pain following a fall or whiplash — do not press or stretch the neck, seek medical assessment.
- NICE NG59, Low back pain and sciatica in over 16s — https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59. Plain-word flag: progressive leg weakness, numbness in the saddle area, or loss of bladder/bowel control with back pain — treat as an emergency, not a self-care case.

**Vascular / cardiac red flags**
- Deep vein thrombosis: one leg (usually calf) swollen, warm, red/discolored, and tender, especially if it came on over hours-to-days without injury — NHS Foundation Trust patient information — https://www.nth.nhs.uk/resources/deep-vein-thrombosis/. Do not massage or cup a limb with these signs; seek same-day medical care.
- Cardiac referred pain: chest discomfort, pressure, or pain spreading to the arm(s), neck, jaw, or upper back, especially with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or lightheadedness — American Heart Association, "Warning Signs of a Heart Attack" — https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack. This overlaps with muscular shoulder/upper-back pain zones our app maps — the app must screen for these accompanying symptoms before treating shoulder/upper-back/arm pain as muscular.

**Contraindications for pressing (manual/trigger-point pressure)**
- Open wound, active skin infection, or rash at the site — do not press directly on it (general massage-therapy contraindication guidance) — https://www.integrativehealthcare.org/mt/14-massage-contraindications-when-to-avoid-modify-treatment/
- Fever or acute systemic viral/bacterial infection — avoid deep pressure until resolved (same source as above).
- Known blood-clotting disorder, on blood-thinning medication, or easy bruising — avoid deep/aggressive pressure — same source.
- Diagnosed osteoporosis, especially moderate-to-severe, or near a fracture site — avoid strong pressure near the spine or affected bones — same source.
- Pregnancy, particularly first trimester, or any suspected/known DVT in the limb — avoid deep pressure — same source.
- Over/near an inflamed or infected joint (e.g. active arthritis flare) — avoid — same source.

**Contraindications for cupping**
- From the cupping systematic reviews above (PMC11955767, PMC12121573) and Chirali's textbook (practitioner-level, cross-checked against the systematic reviews for lay use): skin disease with Koebner phenomenon (e.g. active psoriasis, lichen planus) at the site; broken or very fragile skin; bleeding disorders or anticoagulant medication; pregnancy (avoid abdomen/lower back); very young children (under ~2) and frail elderly (over ~70) as a precaution; directly over varicose veins, recent scar, or known tumor site; active fever/infection. Citations: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11955767/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12121573/

## 5. Copyright Position (general information, not legal advice)

- **Facts and ideas are never protected — only the author's original expression of them is.** This is the core of US copyright law per *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991): "facts do not owe their origin to an act of authorship" and are not copyrightable even when compiled, unless the *selection, coordination, or arrangement* of the facts is itself original. Full opinion: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/499/340/
- **US Copyright Office confirms the same for methods, systems, and facts generally:** Circular 33, "Works Not Protected by Copyright" — https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf. "Copyright does not protect ideas, methods, or systems… copyright protection extends only to the particular expression of an idea and never to the idea itself." A stretching technique, a trigger-point's anatomical location, or the fact that a given muscle refers pain to a given zone is an unprotectable fact/idea.
- **Practical implication for the app:** the app can describe, in its own words, where a trigger point is located and what pain pattern it typically produces, and can draw its own original diagram of the referred-pain zone, because the underlying anatomical/clinical fact is not owned by any publisher. What must NOT be copied: (a) the book's own drawings/illustrations of the body and pain zones (these are original artistic works — redraw independently, don't trace); (b) the book's exact sentences, wording, or distinctive phrasing (copy the fact, not the prose); (c) a book's original selection/arrangement/sequence if it reflects creative judgment (e.g. a uniquely organized muscle-by-muscle treatment protocol structure) — build our own structure driven by our own product logic instead of mirroring a book's chapter/section design.
- **Hong Kong:** Copyright Ordinance (Cap. 528), official text — https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap528. Copyright subsists in "original literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works" (general subsistence provision). Hong Kong law, like the UK's, does not appear to separately *codify* an idea/expression dichotomy in a single numbered section — it operates through the "original work" and "substantial part" infringement tests in case law — **unverified**: no specific HK Ordinance section explicitly stating "ideas are not protected" was located in this pass; treat the fact/expression split as inferred from general copyright principle (consistent with Feist) rather than a citable HK statutory section.
- This is general information only, not legal advice; before publishing redrawn anatomical diagrams commercially, get a qualified IP lawyer to review actual final artwork against the specific source books.
