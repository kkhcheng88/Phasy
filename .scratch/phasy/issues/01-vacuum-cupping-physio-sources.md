# Vacuum cupping in physiotherapy: methods, dose and safety

Type: research
Label: wayfinder:research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

What do physiotherapy (non-TCM) sources say about Vacuum cupping: static and sliding methods, how long and how strong, where it is and is not placed on the ~40 focus muscles (neck, shoulders, upper back, low back, hips), contraindications, adverse events, and the evidence for benefit? Name one or two physiotherapy-framed references to replace the dropped TCM cupping book.

Findings go to `research/vacuum-cupping.md`.

## Answer

Three physiotherapy methods are documented: static cups, sliding cups with lubricant, and cups with active movement ("myofascial decompression"), typically 5–20 minutes depending on method. Avoid bony points, the spine, the front of the neck, lymph nodes, varicose veins and broken skin. Evidence: a 2023 systematic review in sports/musculoskeletal rehab rates low-to-moderate evidence for soft-tissue release and short-term neck/low-back pain relief; a 2024 umbrella review rates overall confidence as low.

Caveats for the Library: placement and frequency guidance (e.g. 1–3 times a week) comes mostly from general safety guides and commercial course sites, not dose studies, so v1 rules should be conservative. No physiotherapy-framed book replaces the dropped TCM book; the peer-reviewed reviews serve as the references instead.
